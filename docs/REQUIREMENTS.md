# EH3D-PHOENIX-PCB: requirements

This register is the single source of the state of each capability. Status values: planned, prototype, implemented, validated. Nineteen requirements (PPCB-001, PPCB-002, PPCB-003, PPCB-007, PPCB-008, PPCB-009, PPCB-011, PPCB-012, PPCB-013, PPCB-014, PPCB-015, PPCB-016, PPCB-017, PPCB-019, PPCB-021, PPCB-022, PPCB-024, PPCB-029 and PPCB-030) are `prototype` and the other 11 are `planned`: none is validated and nothing has been run on real design files. High test coverage is not proof of geometric or electrical correctness, so each requirement says how it will be checked and what result is expected.

| PPCB-001 | Design model with stable object identities and integer nanometre coordinates. | prototype | automatic: property tests over thousands of generated models | Identities survive save, migration and reopening; references never dangle. | Only the foundations exist: checked nanometre and micro-degree values with the 2^30 nm range, orientation predicates in 64-bit integers, arcs as end points plus a sweep, UUID v7 ids from an injectable generator, and canonical JSON. There are no design objects, no references, no project file and no operations yet, so identities across save and migration are untested. |

Schematic and other domains have none. |

| PPCB-024 | RF tools by levels (analytic calculators first). | prototype | automatic plus measurement | Each level states its scope and limits. | Two analytic levels exist. The first is `eh3d-phoenix-stackup` and the command `phoenix stackup` give the impedance, effective permittivity, conductor and dielectric loss, delay and guided wavelength of microstrip, stripline (centred and offset), coplanar waveguide with and without a ground plane and edge-coupled pairs from published quasi-static formulas, and the track width for a target impedance, with the Dk and Df of a layer taken, when asked, from the materials database of EH3D-COMMON at the frequency of the calculation between the points a datasheet publishes (`phoenix stackup --material ID`, `phoenix materials`). Each formula is documented with its source and its range (`docs/STACKUP.md`) and a result outside the range carries a warning. It has not been compared with a field solver or with the measurement of a board; the offset stripline and the coupled pairs are approximations; dispersion, solder mask in the field, even and odd modes, discontinuities and vias are not modelled.
