# EH3D-PHOENIX-PCB: not yet

Good ideas that are not in the first version. Writing them here avoids implementing them early.

| Idea | Why not yet | Revisit when |
|---|---|---|
| Copying a footprint, symbol or device set, moving several items at once and editing a property of many items in the library editor | The editor works on one item at a time; a copy is made by creating and adding | after the window has been used on real libraries |
| Editing polygons, dimensions and frames of a library, and the binary EAGLE libraries | The editor keeps the items it cannot read exactly as the XML they came in and does not read the binary files | when the reader of the binary files exists |
| Footprint generators by density level, more families, and values checked against the standard and the datasheets | The values in `config/footprints.cfg` were entered by hand and are marked as a prototype | when the standard and datasheets of the parts are compared with the generated footprints |
| Replacing device sets by their master and relinking boards and schematics to the replaced items | The editor replaces one footprint or symbol of one library after confirmation; keeping every placed part where it is needs the boards | after the boards are read together with the libraries |
| Differential pairs, length tuning and advanced routing | Basic routing comes first | after the PCB editor works |
| Arcs in the tracks the router makes, and avoiding copper zones | The router makes pieces at 0, 45 and 90 degrees and the rules refuse a track through a zone of another net, but the search does not route around it | when the editor draws zones |
| Push and shove, and routing several tracks at once | The router never moves other copper | after the single track is measured on real boards |
| Through vias and vias between inner layers on boards of more than two layers | A via joins exactly the two layers it is placed between, as the model says | when the stack of vias is part of the model |
| A faster search (a finer mesh only where it is needed, or a search from both ends) | The coarse mesh is simple to reason about and its speed on a large board has not been measured | when a large board is routed and the time is measured |
| Dragging a component with its tracks following | Moving a component leaves its tracks where they are and the ratsnest shows what is left | after the window has been used on real boards |
| Co-simulation of firmware with the circuit | Needs one emulator per processor family, starting with a single one | after circuit simulation |
| Full parametric mechanical CAD | Belongs to EH3D-PHOENIX-CAD and comes after its first steps | after the first mechanical steps |
| Electromagnetic co-simulation and dynamic heat maps | Needs the RF tools and the thermal laboratory | after both exist |
| Plugins loaded at run time | No real third-party need yet; an internal versioned interface comes first | when a third party asks for it |
| Native TLS on the server link | A documented HTTPS proxy is used first | after the secure configuration is tested |
| Clearance between nets of different net classes, and rules per layer pair and per area | The rule file of the second format has net classes with a track width and via limits; a clearance between two classes needs the geometry of the clearance check, and a layer pair or an area has no field in the file | after the clearance check takes a limit for each pair of nets |
| Exact clearance of arcs, round-cornered pads and filled zones | Arcs are judged by their end points and pad shapes by two circles; exact curves need the geometry library that is still to be chosen | after the geometry library is chosen
| Hole-to-copper and hole-to-edge distances and thermal-relief checks | The first set of rules covers sizes, clearances, the edge, drill spacing, mask and silkscreen | when the manufacturing profiles exist
| Frequency-dependent (dispersive) models of the microstrip and coplanar lines | The first calculator is quasi-static and warns when the substrate is a large part of the wavelength | after the stack-up calculator is compared with a field solver |
| Even and odd modes, common-mode impedance and crosstalk of coupled lines | The pair has only the differential impedance of the IPC-2141 fit | after the stack-up calculator is compared with a field solver |
| Solder mask, etch factor and plane cut-outs in the field of a line | The first calculator leaves them out and says so | after the stack-up calculator is compared with a field solver |
| Copper pours, thermal reliefs and clearance around other nets in the Gerber files | A zone is written as its outline; a pour needs the exact geometry library that is still to be chosen | after the geometry library is chosen |
| Polygon pads, round-cornered and chamfered pads, and pads turned by any angle in the Gerber files | The board model has only circle, rectangle and oblong pads and the export flashes a turned pad unrotated unless the angle is a right angle (`PHOENIX-EXPORT-001`) | when the model has pad shapes with outlines and a macro aperture is written |
| Blind and buried vias, back drilling and the blind and buried drill files | The export treats every via as a plated through hole; the model has layer pairs but no drill span rule | after the stack-up has net classes and via spans |
| Object attributes of the Gerber files (aperture functions and the net, component and pin names) | The shared writer writes file attributes only, and the viewers that were meant to check the output are not chosen | when the shared writer writes them and two independent viewers are chosen |
| Plating of slots, castellated holes and press-fit tolerances in the Excellon files | The model does not say which drilled pad is a slot or what its plating is; a drilled oblong pad is a slot only when the profile asks for it | after the pad model has a hole shape and a plating field |
| Pick and place and BOM formats of real machines and assemblers, and importing them | The two CSV files are plain and their columns are a profile setting; no real sample has been compared, and the rule for the rotation of a bottom part is a setting that nobody has confirmed with an assembler | when real samples exist (PPCB-010) |
| Opening the output in two independent Gerber and Excellon viewers and a fabrication review | Until then the state of the export stays at prototype (PPCB-009) | when two viewers are chosen and a manufacturer has looked at a sample |
| Touchstone version 2 keywords, a reference resistance per port and mixed-mode parameters | The reader and writer cover version 1.0 and 1.1 with one reference resistance | when files with those keywords are needed |
| Loss, parasitics and component tolerance in the ladder filters | The filters are ideal and the response is exact for ideal elements | after the stack-up calculator and the RF tools are compared with measurements |
| Noise circles, gain circles and stability circles | The first RF level gives figures (K, mu, gain, VSWR), not circles | after the figures are compared with a network analyser |
| Double stubs, tapered lines and multi-section broadband matching | The first level has the L network, the single stub and the quarter-wave section | after the first level is compared with measurements |
| De-embedding of fixtures and conversion of H and G parameters | Not needed for the first calculators; H and G files are read and refused when they have to be converted | when a measurement needs them |
| Drawing of the Smith chart and of the response curves | The RF tools give the coordinates and the circles; a drawing module belongs to the viewer | after the viewer exists |
| Writing KiCad files (schematics, boards and libraries) | The readers come first and the writer needs the oracle to say that KiCad opens the files (PPCB-013) | after the readers are compared with KiCad on a public corpus |
| KiCad graphics, buses, no-connect flags, the setup of a board, the filled copper of zones, keep-out zones and 3D models in the model | The model holds none of them; the readers count them as left out | when the model has them |
| KiCad pads with rounded corners, trapezoids and custom shapes, oval drills and the plating of a pad | The model has circle, rectangle and oblong pads and a drill diameter; the readers read the rest as the rectangle of its size or a round hole | with the pad shapes of the model |
| Nets of a KiCad schematic by the rules of KiCad (bus members, power flags, no-connect, buses of nets) | The nets are computed from wires, junctions, labels and power symbols and are not compared with KiCad | after the comparison with the oracle |
| Copying, placing new components, the properties of an item and the selection of several items in the editor | The window moves, turns and deletes one item at a time and the properties need a model of attributes | after the window has been seen on a screen and used on real boards |
| A drawn grid in the viewer and an editing of the symbols of a schematic | The grid only snaps the positions of edits, and the model holds no drawing of a symbol | after the prototype has been seen on a screen, and after the symbol library exists |
| Replacing only the primitives of the items that an edit changed | An edit rebuilds the whole scene and the spatial index, which is simple and always right | when the time of an edit on a large board, measured on a real screen, misses the target of `docs/adr/0009-first-interface-stack.md` |
| A canvas on the graphics card (wgpu) and a cache of the triangles of the polygons | The prototype draws through the painter of egui on the processor; only the preparation of the data has been timed | when the frame time measured on a real screen misses the target of `docs/adr/0009-first-interface-stack.md` |
| Drawing of the symbols of a schematic and a view of the board from the bottom | A part is drawn as the box around its pins because the model holds no drawing of a symbol, and the board is drawn from the top only | after the symbol library exists |
| Applying the plan of the library sanitizer (replace the duplicates by the kept item and relink the device sets and the placed parts, with a backup) | The sanitizer only analyses; the replacement must prove first that it changes neither the layout nor the rules: backup with a checked hash, same pads, same names, no turn, same positions and nets, same rule findings, read-back of the written libraries (`docs/SANITIZER.md`) | after the analysis has been run on real libraries and the owner has confirmed groups |
| A redacted form of the sanitizer report | The report names libraries and items because the owner needs them to confirm a group; a mode with only codes, counts and hashes is needed before a report leaves the machine | when a report has to leave the machine |
| Tolerances tuned on real libraries, texts, unusual pad shapes and electrical attributes in the score of the sanitizer | The defaults are guesses checked on libraries made by hand; the score looks at positions, sizes, outline and silkscreen only | after the analysis has been run on real libraries |
| Buses, hierarchical sheets, global labels and power flags in the electrical rule check | The check judges the net list and the loose ends of the wires of one drawing; buses and labels are not in the schematic model | when the schematic model has them |
| A pin type chosen per part, and the pin types of the KiCad files | The type is held per pin of a symbol; the EAGLE reader fills it from the direction of the pins and the KiCad reader gives none, so a KiCad schematic is mostly inconclusive | with the symbol library and the KiCad pin types |
| The matrix of pin types of the reference tool | The default matrix is written by hand from the usual meaning of the types and is not compared with the reference tool | after the check is compared with the reference tool on a corpus (PPCB-006) |
| Net ties, the assignment of a net class to a net in the schematic and the clearance of a class | A class lists its nets by name in the rule file; the schematic has no net class and no net tie | when the schematic model holds them |
| Gate and pin swapping, changes of the pad geometry and moving tracks when the nets of the board change | The synchronisation changes references, values, footprints, parts and the nets of the pads; a renamed net keeps its tracks, a pad moved to another net keeps the tracks it had | after the board editor can reroute |
| The editing mode of the schematic seen and measured on a real screen | The editing mode of the window is written and its state is tested without a screen; the drawing, the mouse, the keys and the speed have never been checked on one | after the stack is decided on a real screen |
| Comparison of the simulator with an existing engine on the reference cases | The tests use the values of the textbooks and the filters of the RF crate; the engine to compare with is not chosen (`docs/adr/0010-own-circuit-simulator.md`) | when an engine is chosen |
| Other semiconductor devices (JFETs, MOSFETs of higher levels, IGBTs, thyristors) and the models of a manufacturer in the simulator | The simulator has diodes, bipolar transistors, MOSFETs of level 1 and op-amp and comparator macromodels of our own, with simple equations (no high injection, no body effect, no sub-threshold current, no slew rate); a library model needs the parameters read and a reference to be compared with | after the simulator is compared with an existing engine |
| Temperature dependence of the devices and a temperature sweep | The temperature is one setting that sets the thermal voltage; the saturation currents and the resistances do not change with it | when a case needs them |
| Transistors and amplifiers in the netlist of a schematic | The netlist reads Q, M and U lines, but a schematic part has no model to name, so the export knows only R, C, L, V, I, D, E, G, F, H and K | when the component database gives models |
| A sparse solver and circuits of thousands of nodes | The solver is dense and the number of unknowns is limited (`max_unknowns`) | when a circuit needs it and the time has been measured |
| A command for the noise analysis, flicker noise, noise referred to the input and the noise of the op-amp | The noise at an output (thermal and shot) is in the library only | when a case needs them |
| Sensitivity, parameter sweeps (`.step`) and measurements (`.measure`) in the simulator | Only the operating point, the AC sweep, the noise at an output and the transient exist | after the reference cases pass |
| A waveform viewer for the results of the simulator | The results are CSV and JSON tables; drawing belongs to the viewer | after the viewer exists |
| Ideal edges (a rise or fall time of zero) in the adaptive step, switches and the control of the step by the charge of the junctions | An ideal edge is evaluated at the end of the step that reaches it, so that step shrinks to its minimum and is noted. The adaptive step also stops at the corners of the sources and compares one step with two half steps, but a corner that comes from a device (a switch, a comparator output that is not driven by a source) is not known beforehand | when switching circuits are simulated |
| Parameters of subcircuits, `.include`, `.lib`, `.global` and the other models of the SPICE language in the netlist reader | The reader has `.param`, `.model` of diodes and `.subckt` without parameters, and says what it leaves out | when netlists written for other programs are imported |
| Simulation of schematics with integrated circuits, connectors and parts that have a model of their own | The export knows the parts whose designator is one letter of the supported kinds and lists the parts it left out | when the component database gives models |
| Selecting objects by pointing at a coordinate in a script, and the commands that need a window | The interpreter names objects (a reference designator, a signal) and has no drawing to point at; `MOVE (x y) (x y)`, `DELETE (x y)` and `GROUP` with a rectangle are pending with their cause | when the editor has a window |
| Curved wires, mirrored placement and creating layers from a script | The reader does not take a bend between two points of `WIRE`, a mirrored rotation changes the side of a component and the board has no operation to add a layer | after the editor and the board operations for them exist |
| The rest of the commands of the original language (`ADD`, `ROUTE`, `AUTO`, `COPY`, `PAD`, `SIGNAL`, ...) | They are known and listed in `known_pending` of `config/script.cfg`, and each needs an editor, a library or the router; the report lists them as pending | command by command, as the engines for them exist |
| Comparing the script interpreter and its report with the original tool, on the author's own scripts and jobs | Everything is built from the public description of the language and tested with scripts written for it; the equivalences are claims about the design, and the gate of phase F8 needs the rate on the real scripts | when the corpus and the golden files of EH3D-VALIDATION-LAB exist |
| Scripts that edit a schematic | The schematic has reversible operations now (`docs/SCHEMATIC_EDIT.md`) but the interpreter does not drive them, so only `GRID`, `DISPLAY`, `SET`, `ERC` and `SCRIPT` apply to a schematic | when the commands of the interpreter are mapped onto the operations of the editor |
| Running an output job to make its files, and several layers in one Gerber file | `phoenix cam-import` only reads a job into an export profile; the export writes one file per layer with one extension, and the format of the job is read from its public description and not checked against a file saved by the original tool | after the export can write several layers in one file and a job has been compared with the original tool |
| A sandbox for scripts in a separate low-privilege process, with limits on time and memory and a log with the hash of the script | The restrictions (no program, relative paths inside allowed folders, limits on size and operations) are checks inside the program, as `docs/DECISIONS.md` says a child process alone is not a boundary | before a script found in a project can be run at all |
| Checking the RF structures with an electromagnetic simulator and with a measurement | The dimensions come from closed formulas of the books and papers, tested with the values they print; the steps, T junctions, corners, open-end coupling and vias are not modelled and the coupled-microstrip model is marked prototype | when a field solver is chosen and a board with a structure has been measured |
| Clearance between copper polygons in the rule check | The structures are drawn as polygons of copper (zones); the rules judge tracks, vias and pads, and of zones only the clearance written in them and the overlap of zones of different nets, so the gaps between resonators are not judged | when the rules read the outline of a zone |
| More RF structures: stripline, coplanar waveguide and inner-layer structures, hairpin and interdigital filters, rat-race and coupled-line couplers, a Klopfenstein taper, a compensated T junction, and tuning on the response | The generator draws the structures of one family on an outer microstrip layer from closed formulas and does not tune them | when a case needs them and a simulator can tune them |
| Land patterns of connectors from data sheets, and the isolation resistor and other parts placed from a library | The SMA launch and the Wilkinson divider use generic sizes from `config/rfgen.cfg` and leave a gap for the resistor; the board has no library of footprints to draw from | after the component database and the footprint library exist |
| The rest of the schematic editor: the drawing of symbols, hierarchical sheets, buses as objects of their own, automatic renumbering of references, cutting and joining a piece of a wire, the following of the bends of a wire and of labels when a part moves, turning a group about a common centre, adding and removing sheets in the window, and a footprint chosen in the editor | The operations of `docs/SCHEMATIC_EDIT.md` work on parts, whole wires, labels and power ports; a wire that ends on a moved pin follows it and keeps its pieces horizontal or vertical, nothing else follows; a bus is a list of names; a part is the box around its pins; the file of operations writes an EAGLE schematic without footprints because it has no board | next, as the following step of the editor |
