# EH3D-PHOENIX-PCB: advanced design rule checks

**State: prototype.** The checks run beside the rule engine (`eh3d-phoenix-rules`) and its live check in the board editor; they add codes and change none of the existing ones. They have been tried on boards made by hand and by the tests only. Nothing here has been compared with the rules of a manufacturer or with another tool, and the limits in `config/drc_advanced.cfg` are examples, not anybody's rules.

## What is checked

| Code | Rule | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| PHOENIX-DRC-001 | `annular_ring_min` | The ring of a via (pad diameter minus drill, halved) and of a drilled pad (its smaller side minus the drill, halved). |
| PHOENIX-DRC-002 | `copper_edge` | The gap between a track or a via and the outline of the board. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-003 | `pad_edge` | The gap between a pad and the outline of the board. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-004 | `drill_class` | The drill of a via or a drilled pad against the minimum of the class of its net, or the default minimum. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-005 | `silk_hole` | The gap between silkscreen and the drill of a through hole (drilled pads and vias through the whole stack). |
| PHOENIX-DRC-006 | `neck` | A track narrower than `neck_min_width_nm` that joins, at one end, wider copper of its net (a pad, a via or a wider track). |
| PHOENIX-DRC-007 | `acid_trap` | The angle between two pieces of tracks of one net that leave the same point. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-008 | `isolation` | The gap between copper of two nets that have a voltage between them, against the table of spacing. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-009 | `island` | Tracks, vias and zones of a net that are joined to each other but to no pad. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-010 | `stub` | A track with one free end and one joined end. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-011 | `via_duplicate` | Two vias that share a layer and are at most `via_duplicate_max_distance_nm` apart (0: the same place). |
| PHOENIX-DRC-012 | `pad_no_net` | A pad of a placed component that is on no net. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-013 | `outside_outline` | A component with a pad whose centre is outside the outline. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-014 | `advanced_not_judged` | What could not be judged (information, in the inconclusive list). |
| PHOENIX-DRC-015 | | A settings file that is not valid. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-016 | `short_track` | A track that is no longer than `short_track_min_nm` in all (a fragment left over from editing). A track with an arc is not judged. Off until its level is given. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-017 | `track_angle` | The straight pieces of a track that are off the grid of angles of `track_angle_step_deg` (45: the eight directions; 90: along the two axes). One finding for each track, with the number of pieces. Off until its level is given. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-018 | `ring_class` | The ring of a via and of a drilled pad against the `ring_min_nm` of the class of its net. Off until its level is given. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-019 | `via_count` | A net with more vias than `via_count_max`, one finding for each net. Off until its level is given. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-020 | `outline_corner` | A corner of the outline or of a cutout that is sharper than `outline_corner_min_angle_deg`, written `outline corner N` or `cutout K corner N` with its angle in the detail. The two corners at the ends of an arc of the outline and the corners next to an edge with no length are not judged. Off until its level is given. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-021 | `net_name` | The spelling of the names of the nets, read by `lint_net_names`: an empty name, a space, a character that is not a letter, a digit or one of `_-+.$/#~!*<>[]@%`, a name longer than `net_name_max_len`, two names that differ only in case, a name a program made up (`N$12`, `Net-(R1-Pad1)`) when `net_name_autogenerated` is `on`, and the half of a pair (`_P`/`_N`, `_DP`/`_DN`) whose other half is missing when `net_name_pairs` is `on`. One finding for each thing, written `net NAME`, with the kind and its detail. Off until its level is given. |
| PHOENIX-DRC-022 | `mask_sliver` | A strip of solder mask between two openings of one layer of mask that is more than nothing and less than `mask_sliver_min_nm`, read by `mask_slivers`. The opening over a pad is its box (turned by its angle) grown by `mask_sliver_expansion_nm` on every side. A pad at a quarter turn that is a rectangle with square corners is exact and gives a proven finding; a round pad, an oblong pad or a rectangle with round corners is taken as its box, which can only make the strip look narrower, so its pairs are told as information in the inconclusive list, and a pad at another angle is not judged (`PHOENIX-DRC-014`). Off until its level is given. |

The existing rules keep their own codes (`PHOENIX-CHECK-001` to `015`). Where an advanced check looks like one of them (the annular ring, the distance to the edge, the smallest drill) it has its own limit and its own level in `config/drc_advanced.cfg` and is reported under its own code, so the two never hide each other.

## Settings: `config/drc_advanced.cfg`

Lines are `key = value`; blank lines and lines starting with `#` are ignored. The file is strict: a key the program does not know, a key written twice (`iso_row` is the only one that repeats), a value out of range and a file larger than 1 MiB are refused with `PHOENIX-DRC-015` and the number of the line. A line that is left out keeps the value of the file built into the program.

* `<check>_level = off | info | warning | error` for each check. A level of `off` turns the check off; only `error` makes `phoenix check` exit with 1. The levels are the severity of the findings, so a limit can be reported without failing the board.
* The limits are in nanometres (`annular_ring_min_nm`, `copper_edge_min_nm`, `pad_edge_min_nm`, `silk_hole_min_nm`, `neck_min_width_nm`, `via_duplicate_max_distance_nm`, `drill_default_min_nm`) and in degrees (`acid_trap_min_angle_deg`).
* Drill classes: `drill_class.NAME.nets = A, B` and `drill_class.NAME.min_nm = N`. A net that is in no class has the default minimum; a net in two classes is refused. A class may also give a ring: `drill_class.NAME.ring_min_nm = N` (0, or no line, gives none), which `ring_class_level` judges for the vias and the drilled pads of its nets; the nets of a class with no ring are left to `annular_ring_min_nm`. A class that gives a ring and no drill has no minimum drill of its own, so its nets are not judged by `drill_class_level`.
* `copper_edge_layer.NAME = N` gives the layer called NAME (in any case) a distance from copper to the board edge of its own, in nanometres, in place of `copper_edge_min_nm`; a via takes the greatest limit of the layers it joins, a layer without a line counting as the default, and the pads are not changed (`pad_edge_min_nm`). A name that is not a layer of the board changes nothing.
* `net_name_level` and `mask_sliver_level` are two more levels, `off` in the file that comes with the program. `net_name_max_len` (0 to 1000, 0 does not judge the length) and the switches `net_name_autogenerated` and `net_name_pairs` (`on` or `off`) are the limits of the names; `mask_sliver_min_nm` (0 does not judge it) and `mask_sliver_expansion_nm` are the limits of the mask. The 100000 of `mask_sliver_min_nm` is the figure of the profile `config/dfm_jlcpcb.cfg`; take the one of the maker that makes the board.
* The five further checks have a level each (`short_track_level`, `track_angle_level`, `ring_class_level`, `via_count_level`, `outline_corner_level`), `off` in the file that comes with the program, with their limits `short_track_min_nm`, `track_angle_step_deg` (45 or 90), `via_count_max` (0 to 1000000; 0 does not judge it) and `outline_corner_min_angle_deg` (0 to 180; 0 does not judge it).
* `voltage.NET = V` gives the voltage of a net, in volts. A net that has no voltage is not judged by the spacing check; write the ground net as `voltage.GND = 0`.
* `grid_cell_nm` is the size of the cell of the search grid. It changes the speed only.

## Spacing by voltage

The check takes the difference of the voltages of two nets, picks the row of the table whose highest voltage is not below it, reads the spacing of the column `iso_column` and compares it with the gap between the copper of the two nets on a shared layer. Above the last row the spacing is the figure per volt of `iso_per_volt_nm` times the voltage. The gap is measured on the surface of one layer; it is a clearance for copper on the same layer and not a creepage path through the board, and nothing is computed between layers.

The table in the file is Table 6-1, Electrical Conductor Spacing, of IPC-2221 (Generic Standard on Printed Board Design), with the columns B1 (internal conductors), B2 (external, uncoated, sea level to 3050 m), B3 (external, uncoated, above 3050 m), B4 (external, permanent polymer coating), A5 (external, conformal coating over the assembly), A6 (external component lead or termination, uncoated) and A7 (external component lead or termination, conformal coating). **The figures were written from memory and have not been verified against the standard.** Check each one against your copy of the standard before you rely on it; every finding of this check says so. The standard is not part of this repository and no part of its text is copied.

## Method

* All arithmetic on distances is exact integer arithmetic on nanometres, as in the rule engine. A shape that is too coarse to tell (a pad that is not round is read as its inscribed and its circumscribed circle) or numbers too large for the exact test go to the inconclusive list and never turn into a failure or a pass. Arcs in tracks are measured by their end points: a distance found that way is proven, a distance not found says nothing about the arc.
* The angle of a corner of the outline is worked out the same way, from the exact integer cross and dot products of the two edges, and compared with a tolerance of a billionth of a degree: a right angle is not sharper than 90 degrees.
* The acid trap angle is worked out in floating point from the exact integer directions of the pieces and compared with a tolerance of a billionth of a degree; an exact right angle is therefore not an acute one. Only straight pieces are looked at, and only where two pieces share an end point: a track that ends on the middle of another is not seen.
* A stub is judged only when the other end is joined. A track with both ends free is an island and is reported by the island check.
* Pairs of items that may be close are found with a uniform grid. The grid chooses which pairs are compared and the same exact test decides, so the report equals the one of the search that compares every pair (`IndexMode::Naive`); the tests check it on boards of 170 items with several cell sizes. Items that cover too many cells are kept apart and compared with all.
* The report is in a fixed order (rule, place, position), so the same board and settings give the same bytes.

## Reports

`phoenix check --advanced [--drc-config FILE] [--json | --sarif] [--lang xx] <board>` runs the rule engine and then the advanced checks and prints one report. `--drc-config FILE` implies `--advanced`. Three forms:

* text, with the titles in `en`, `es`, `de`, `fr`, `it`, `ja`, `zh` or `pt` (Portuguese of Portugal), the confirmed and the inconclusive findings in two lists;
* JSON, the same two lists with the codes (`phoenix-check/1`);
* `--sarif`, a JSON layout like SARIF named `phoenix-drc/1`: `rules` (the codes that appear, with name and title), and `results` with `ruleId`, `level` (`note`, `warning` or `error`), `kind` (`fail` for a proven finding, `review` for an inconclusive one), `message`, `location` (the name and `at_nm`) and `properties` (`measured_nm`, `limit_nm`, `detail`). It is not the OASIS SARIF and no SARIF reader has been tried on it.

Exit code: 0 when nothing is proven wrong, 1 when a finding of level `error` is proven, 2 when the input is unusable.

### Tables by category

`--csv FILE` and `--html FILE` (of `phoenix check` and `phoenix connectivity`, with any of the forms above) also write the report for people who sort it in a spreadsheet or read it in a browser. Each writes a new file: a file that exists is never replaced, two outputs may not share a name, and nothing is written when one of them is refused (exit code 2).

* The CSV header is `list,category,severity,code,title,location,x_nm,y_nm,measured_nm,limit_nm,detail`. One row per finding, the proven ones first (`list` is `confirmed` or `inconclusive`). `category`, `severity` and `list` keep their English keys in every language so a script can read them; only `title` is in the language of `--lang`. A text cell that a spreadsheet would run as a formula gets a quote mark in front, and a cell with a comma, a quote or a line break is quoted.
* The HTML page has no script and no outside file and escapes every text that comes from the board. It starts with a summary of the proven and inconclusive findings of each category, then a table for each category that has findings. The headings are in the language of `--lang`.
* The categories group the codes by what is fixed together: `size` (widths, via sizes, drills, rings, necks, short tracks), `clearance` (copper clearance, isolation by voltage), `edge` (the board edge, sharp corners of the outline and drill spacing), `mask_silk` (solder mask and silkscreen), `zones`, `connectivity`, `advanced` (islands, stubs, duplicate vias, acid traps and the other advanced checks), `capability` (the checks against the profile of a maker, `docs/DFM.md`) and `other` (what could not be judged). The category is a reading aid and changes no result.


`--time` (also of `phoenix connectivity`) writes one more line to the error stream, `time: S.SSS s`, with the seconds the command took from the end of the options to the end of the report, to three decimals. The report on the output and the exit code are the same with and without it. It is a wall-clock time of one run on the machine it ran on, so it is for comparing two runs on one computer and not a figure of the program.

## In the board editor

The key `advanced_drc` of `config/boardedit.cfg` (off by default) adds the proven findings of the advanced checks, with the built-in settings, to the check the editor makes before and after every operation, so `added` and `removed` of the report of an operation include them and an operation that adds a finding of level `error` is refused when `reject_new_errors` is on. With the key off the behaviour of the editor is the one it had before.

## Two library functions beside the checks

* `lint_net_names` reads a list of names of nets and lists what is wrong with the spelling of each: empty (`empty`), a space (`space`), a character that is not a letter, a digit or one of `_-+.$/#~!*<>[]@%` (`bad_character`), more characters than `max_len` (`too_long`), a name listed twice (`duplicate`), two names that differ only in case (`case_clash`), a name that a program made up such as `N$12`, `Net-(R1-Pad1)` or `unconnected-(U1-NC)` (`autogenerated`) and the half of a pair (`_P` and `_N`, `_DP` and `_DN`, in any case) whose other half is not in the list (`pair_missing`). The issues are ordered by name and then by kind, so the same list gives the same answer whatever its order; the limits are the fields of `NetLintConfig` (length 32, autogenerated names and pairs listed).
* `mask_slivers` reads the rectangles of the solder mask openings of one side, with the expansion over the pad already added, and lists each pair whose gap is more than nothing and less than the narrowest strip of mask that may be left. The gap is worked out as a square in integer nanometres, so that the corners of two openings are compared by their true distance and nothing overflows at the ends of the range; openings that touch or overlap are one opening and give nothing. Neither function reads a board file, and neither is a rule of the checks above yet.

## Not done

* Thermal reliefs and the distance from a plated hole to copper of another net on the layers without its pad (the zone outlines against the edge, holes that are not plated and net classes are judged by the ordinary rule check).
* Creepage along a path (slots, cut-outs, the surface of a coating) and clearance in air between layers.
* A neck inside a zone, or along a track between two obstacles.
* Voltages by net class or from the schematic; the voltage is a list of names in the settings file.
* A check of the IPC-2221 figures against the standard, and of the whole set against a manufacturer's rules or another tool.
