# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here.

## [Unreleased]

- Leave out the small islands of a pour in the Gerber files: `gerber.zone_island_min_area_nm2` (0, the default, writes every zone) joins the zones of one net and one layer that share a stretch of border into islands, compares the area of each (the outlines less the cutouts) with it and does not write a smaller island with its clearing; the check of the export counts what is written, and the new `PHOENIX-EXPORT-021` says what was left out
- Give the thermal relief to pads that are not round and allow two spokes: `gerber.zone_thermal_spokes` accepts 0, 2 or 4, `gerber.zone_thermal_non_round` (false, the default) adds the rectangular and oblong pads at a right angle, with the gap and the spoke width of the profile; a pad turned by another angle stays solid and is reported with the new `PHOENIX-EXPORT-022`; both codes have their titles in the eight languages
- Add `tools/make_shots.py`, which takes the pictures of the windows of `phoenix-gui` for each version with the picture mode (one run for each window, an example board of the repository, a settings folder of its own, a time limit, a check that each PNG is not empty or of one colour and weighs less than 400 KB) and its tests in `tools/test_make_shots.py`, with the way to renew them in `docs/VIEWER.md`
- Add the picture mode to `phoenix-gui`: `--shot FILE.png` with `--size WxH`, `--theme`, `--wait N`, `--window NAME` (the start screen, the document, the 3D window, the panel, the imported Gerber files or a window of the toolbox) and `--open FILE` opens the window, saves a PNG file of it and closes by itself, reads and writes no preferences and no list of recent files, and refuses to write over a file unless `--replace` is given (`docs/VIEWER.md`)
- Add named templates to the panel settings: the lines under `[template NAME]` in `config/panel.cfg` are applied after the base lines with `phoenix panel --template NAME`, and ignored otherwise
- Import the bill of materials of another program into `phoenix bom`: `--import FILE` with a map of columns in `config/bom_import.cfg` (delimiter, column names, several references in one cell, fitted and not-to-fit columns) fills the maker, the part number and the parts that are not fitted, reports a quantity that is not the number of references, a reference in two lines and the parts the bill and the board disagree about (`PHOENIX-BOM-019` to `021`), and `--parts-out FILE` writes the data as a file of part data
- Check a board against the capability profile of a maker: `phoenix check --dfm FILE` reads the limits of the rule check, the most copper layers, the least and greatest size of the board, the largest drill and the most holes from a text file (`docs/DFM.md`, `PHOENIX-DFM-001` to `006`); the profile that comes with the program is an example with invented figures and says it is not verified
- Write the report of a check as a table and as a page: `--csv FILE` and `--html FILE` of `phoenix check` and `phoenix connectivity` group the findings by category (sizes, clearances, the edge and the holes, mask and silkscreen, zones, connectivity, advanced checks), keep the English keys in the CSV and put the headings of the page in the eight languages, escape every text of the board and never replace a file
- Choose the grouping and the columns of the bill of materials in `config/costs.cfg`: `group_by` leaves the maker or the part number out of the grouping, and `csv_columns` names the columns of the CSV file and their order
- Carry the openings of the boards and the cutouts of their zones into the panel: every copy has them moved, turned and mirrored as the copy is, the routed outline of the panel (`panel_profile.gbr`) draws them as closed contours, `phoenix dxf-export panel` draws them as polygons of the layer of the outline, and the layer files clear the cutouts of the zones (the function `panel_openings` and `panel_files_with_cutouts`)
- Name the new steps in the history of the window in the eight languages: the outline, the openings inside it, a track with an arc, tracks pushed aside, a pair, the autorouter and the change of a zone are named from what the step does, with tests of the texts and of the names
- Write the cutouts of a zone in the KiCad, EAGLE and Specctra files: KiCad rule areas that keep the pour out and allow tracks, vias and pads (read back as cutouts of the zones of their layer that contain them, any other keep-out area is counted as lost), EAGLE restrict areas on the layers 41 and 42 (read back as cutouts of the pours of the layer, a cutout on an inner layer or with fewer than three points reported as not written), and Specctra keep-out areas on the layer of the zone with a note that they keep out tracks and vias too; the file of the export returns its notes and `phoenix autoroute` prints them
- Read the buses of EAGLE schematics into the bus and bus entry objects: a vector in the name of a `bus` (`D[0..7]`, `A[0..3],B[0..3]`, `BUS:A[0..3]`) is a bus of the sheet anchored where its first wire starts and expanded to its members, a net of the sheet with the name of a member that ends on a wire of the bus is an entry, what the model cannot say is counted, and the EAGLE writer reports the buses and entries it does not write
- Use a via of the net that is already on the board, on a node of the grid, as a free change of layer in the autorouter, and route differential pairs that are asked for (`--pairs`, `--pair-config`, `autoroute_with_pairs`) as two tracks side by side on one layer when there is room, with the code `PHOENIX-AROUTE-017` and the reason in the report when a pair is routed as two ordinary nets
- Add the thermal relief to the pour of the export: `gerber.zone_thermal_spokes` (0 or 4), `gerber.zone_thermal_gap_nm` and `gerber.zone_thermal_spoke_width_nm` clear four gaps around each round pad of the net of a zone that lies inside it, with clear polarity and the pad and the spokes left as copper
- Add tests of these changes with the numbers worked out by hand: the cutouts of a panel with a turn and a mirror, the profile and the drawing of the openings, the names of the steps of the history in every language, the round trip of the cutouts of a zone through KiCad and EAGLE, the keep-out areas of the Specctra file, the buses and entries of an EAGLE schematic, the free change of layer and the coupled pair with the points of both tracks, and the corners of the gaps of a thermal relief
- Add the crate `eh3d-phoenix-field2d` and the command `phoenix field2d <config|board> [--stackup FILE] [--net NAME] [--settings FILE] [--freq-ghz GHZ] [--refine N] [--out FILE] [--json] [--lang CODE]`: a two-dimensional field solver for the cross section of a line (microstrip, stripline, coplanar line with or without a plane, and pairs). The mesh is rectangular and not uniform, with a line at every edge and cells that start fine at the edge of a conductor and grow by a ratio; the permittivity and the loss tangent of each layer are taken at the frequency from the stack-up; the Laplace equation is solved by finite volumes with conjugate gradients or successive over-relaxation, with limits of cells and iterations (a solution that does not reach its tolerance gives an error and no number). From the capacitance with and without the dielectric it gives the impedance, effective permittivity, inductance, delay and dielectric loss and, for a pair, the even and odd modes, the differential and common impedance and the mutual capacitance and inductance, beside the closed formulas of `phoenix stackup` with the difference in percent. A dry run unless `--out` names a new file. Settings with strict keys in `config/field2d.cfg`; codes `PHOENIX-FIELD-001` to `PHOENIX-FIELD-013` in the eight languages (`docs/FIELD2D.md`)
- Add the option `--field2d` to `phoenix stackup`, which solves the same lines with the field solver and adds the result and the difference from the closed formulas to the report (a key `field2d` in the JSON)
- Add the heating of tracks and vias: the module `heating` of the stack-up crate and the command `phoenix thermal --currents FILE [--stackup FILE] [--net NAME] [--settings FILE] [--out FILE] [--json] [--lang CODE] <board>`, which reads the current of each net from a file of `net = amps` lines and gives the rise of each track and via by the fit of IPC-2221 and by the convection model of the thermal laboratory (the resistance growing with the temperature, a closed form of the same fixed point), with a warning and the exit code 1 above a limit and the codes `PHOENIX-HEAT-001` to `PHOENIX-HEAT-012`; the constants of the fit are the ones usually quoted and IPC-2152 is not applied. Settings in `config/heating.cfg` (`docs/THERMAL.md`)
- Round the corners of the zone fill: the option `corner_radius_nm` with `corner_tolerance_nm` in `config/zones.cfg` (zero, the default, leaves every corner sharp) rounds with arcs of integer arithmetic the corners of the fill that face outwards and the corners of the gaps of a thermal relief, which gives the spokes a fillet at their root; the rounded fill takes copper only from itself or adds it inside the gap of a pad of its own net, and `phoenix zones check` measures it like any other fill (`docs/ZONES.md`)
- Add five reference cases and two pending ones in `tests/oracle/field.json`: the field solver against the capacitor of parallel plates, a coaxial line drawn on the mesh and the exact stripline of zero thickness, and the rise by convection and the resistance per millimetre of a track against their closed forms; the pending cases of the printed chart of IPC-2221 say that the fit exists now and that its constants are not checked against the standard
- Add tests of these changes with the numbers worked out by hand: the exactness of the mesh for parallel plates and capacitors in series, the fall of the error as the mesh is refined, the agreement with the stripline of Cohn and the microstrip of Hammerstad and Jensen, the equality of conjugate gradients and over-relaxation, the limits of cells and iterations, the rises of tracks by the fit and by the convection model, the fillets of a right-angle, an acute and an obtuse corner, the corners that are shared or lie on the side of another piece, the rounded fill measured by the check, and the commands through `run`
- Add `outline_cutouts` to the board model: the openings inside the outline, each a closed polygon, optional and not written when empty; the operation `set_outline_cutouts` (reversible), a check of each polygon (`PHOENIX-BOARD-006`), the edge clearance of the rules, the edge of the zones of the editor and the advanced checks (`copper_edge`, `pad_edge`, `outside_outline`) take their edge from the outline and from every cutout; `phoenix dxf-import` keeps the cutouts of a drawing instead of listing them (one undo takes outline and cutouts back) and `phoenix dxf-export` writes each as a closed contour, the Gerber profile has a closed contour for the outline and for each cutout, and the KiCad (`Edge.Cuts`), EAGLE (layer 20), Specctra (`keepout`) and viewer drawings carry them; the code `PHOENIX-DXF-015` is now an information
- Add `roundness` and `plated` to the pads of the board model, optional with the old meaning as the default: a rectangular pad with rounded corners (EAGLE `roundness`, KiCad `roundrect` and `roundrect_rratio`) is read and written by both formats, the Gerber files write it as a region of straight pieces at a right angle (copper, solder mask and paste), a drilled pad that is not plated (EAGLE `hole`, KiCad `np_thru_hole`) has no copper and no annular ring in the rules, no pad on the copper layers and goes to the Excellon file of the holes that are not plated whatever layers it is on; the lists of what the KiCad reader simplifies no longer name these two
- Write the zones of a copper layer with clear polarity where copper is taken away: after the region of each zone the Gerber files carry, as objects of clear polarity (`LPC`, then `LPD` again before the tracks, vias and pads), the `cutouts` of the zone (a new optional field of the zone of the model) and a keep-away of its clearance around every track, via and pad of another net on the layer; the check of the export and the round trip count dark objects only, and the aperture of a keep-away has the function of the object it keeps away from

- Add the crate `eh3d-phoenix-autoroute` and the command `phoenix autoroute native [--nets N1,N2] [--layers L1,L2] [--config FILE] [--out FILE] <board>`: an autorouter that needs no outside program. A routing grid is derived from the rules, a map of the obstacles of each copper layer (pads, vias, tracks, the board edge and the zones of other nets) uses the spatial index, an A* search per layer with vias has costs for length, corners, vias and steps against the preferred direction of a layer, and rip-up and reroute works within limits that count nodes and passes; the order of the nets is shortest, widest or critical first. The result is one reversible all-or-nothing plan (also as a group of operations of the board editor) that is judged by the design rules before and after and never adds a rule error, with a report that is the same for the same input (nets completed, connections left unrouted with a reason, vias, length, rip-ups, expanded nodes). It is a dry run unless `--out` names a new file; the settings are in `config/autoroute_native.cfg` with strict keys, the codes `PHOENIX-AROUTE-001` to `016` have titles in the eight languages, and `docs/AUTOROUTE_NATIVE.md` describes it
